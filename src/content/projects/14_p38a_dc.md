## Two cytokines, one decision

- **Dendritic cells** show antigens to T cells and start the immune response. The **pDC** subset is the one that pours out interferons.
- They are built in the bone marrow under two cytokines: **Flt3L**, the main one, and **M-CSF**, a back-up that can stand in for it.
- A progenitor cell has to pick. Nobody knew **what does the picking**.

## Delete it, and the hierarchy inverts

- Mice without p38α in their blood cells lose Nedd4, and **CD115 piles up** on their progenitors.
- The receptor's **mRNA never changes**: the protein simply stops being destroyed.
- The progenitors now listen to **M-CSF** instead of Flt3L.

## What the single-cell map showed

- 18,113 bone marrow progenitors, wild type and mutant, mapped onto the whole developmental tree.
- The early progenitors shrank, the myeloid ones expanded, and the **pDC branch collapsed**; the trajectory toward mature pDCs **stalled** part-way.
- Among all the genes that changed, **Nedd4** stood out as the one falling across the entire pDC branch. That is how the mechanism was found.

## Quantity restored, quality lost

- The detour works, numerically: mutant progenitors make **two to ten times** as many pDCs in culture and after transplant.
- But the cells are **hollow**. They lose **IL-12** across every subset, and **type I and III interferons** in pDCs and cDC1s.
- Counting cells would have called these mice nearly normal.

## The cost, in a tumor

- Mice lacking p38α in blood cells, or only in dendritic cells, **cannot control a melanoma**.
- Tumors fill with fewer **CD8 T cells**, and fewer of them are armed.
- The T cells themselves are fine: injecting healthy dendritic cells into the tumor **partly rescues** it. The failure is in priming.

## Why it matters

- It had long been known that **M-CSF can replace Flt3L** in mice that lack it. This gives that observation a mechanism.
- It separates the **quantity** of dendritic cells from their **quality**, and shows a compensation that hides a defect instead of fixing it.
- p38α therefore sits upstream of how well the immune system can be primed against a tumor.

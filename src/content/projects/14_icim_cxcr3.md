## When immunotherapy attacks the heart

- Checkpoint inhibitors can inflame the heart muscle. **Myocarditis** is rare but can be **fatal**, and it is driven by CD8<sup>+</sup> T cells that should not be there.
- The standard answer is **steroids**. They calm the heart, but they may also blunt the anti-tumor response the patient needed in the first place.
- Treating the side effect and treating the cancer pull in **opposite directions**.

## A drug that does not trade one for the other

- Blocking **CXCR3**, the receptor that guides activated T cells into inflamed tissue, **reduced myocarditis** and lowered **troponin I**, the blood marker of heart injury.
- At the same time it **improved tumor control**, rather than costing it.
- The puzzle: how can one blockade help the heart *and* the tumor, when the same cells do both jobs?

## It empties the heart, not the tumor

- After CXCR3 blockade, the heart holds **fewer** CD8<sup>+</sup> T cells and fewer **CXCR3<sup>+</sup>** CD8<sup>+</sup> T cells.
- T cell accumulation **in the tumor is untouched**.
- So the drug is not simply suppressing T cells. Something about the two destinations differs.

## Two tissues, two metabolic programs

- Single-cell profiling of **heart, tumor and blood** compared the *Cxcr3*-high CD8<sup>+</sup> T cells in each organ.
- The **tumor** cells were enriched for **hypoxia**, **glycolysis**, **mTORC1 signaling** and **IFN-γ response**, relative to their cardiac counterparts.
- The same cell type, by surface marker, was running a different metabolism depending on where it sat.

## The same drug, opposite effects

- In **cardiac** *Cxcr3*-high CD8<sup>+</sup> T cells, CXCR3 blockade **suppressed** glycolysis and **IFN-γ**.
- In **tumor** *Cxcr3*-high precursor-exhausted cells, it **raised** them.
- One receptor, one antagonist, and the effector program falls in one organ while it rises in the other.

## Oxygen is the difference

- Staining for tissue oxygen confirmed what the single-cell programs implied: **tumors are hypoxic**, the **heart is oxygen-rich**.
- So the test became a question about air: culture CD8<sup>+</sup> T cells lacking *Cxcr3* at **21% oxygen** and at **3% oxygen**, and see whether oxygen alone flips the response.

Blocking mTORC1 directly reproduced the pattern, placing **mTORC1 downstream of CXCR3**.

## Why it matters

- The toxicity and the cure are not inseparable. They can be **uncoupled**, if the intervention exploits a difference between the two tissues.
- Here that difference is **oxygen**, which decides whether a blocked T cell loses its effector program or finds a way around it.
- A route to **safer immunotherapy**: suppress the attack on the heart without disarming the attack on the tumor.

## Repeats are hard to screen for

- **Repeats** are stretches of a genome that occur again and again. They mark places where biology breaks its own rules, such as **CRISPR** arrays and **mobile elements**.
- Existing tools each find **one type** of repeat. A cheap, **generic** screen was missing.
- D2R gives any DNA sequence **one number** for how repetitive it is, whatever the type of repeat.

## Idea: compare a sequence with itself

A **k-mer** is a short word of length $k$. A repetitive sequence contains the **same k-mers again and again**, so count how often a word **matches itself**.

<div class="seq" role="img" aria-label="The sequence ACGGTACGGTACGGT, with the three occurrences of the word ACG highlighted."><i class="m">A</i><i class="m">C</i><i class="m">G</i><i class="">G</i><i class="">T</i><i class="m">A</i><i class="m">C</i><i class="m">G</i><i class="">G</i><i class="">T</i><i class="m">A</i><i class="m">C</i><i class="m">G</i><i class="">G</i><i class="">T</i></div>

With $k = 3$, the word **ACG** occurs $X_{\mathrm{ACG}} = 3$ times, giving $3 \times 2 = 6$ **matching pairs**.

## The statistic

$$
D_2^R \;=\; \frac{\displaystyle\sum_{w}\Big( X_w\,(X_w-1) \;-\; \mathbb{E}\big[X_w\,(X_w-1)\big] \Big)}{n\,(n-1)}
$$

- $X_w(X_w-1)$ counts the **matching pairs** of word $w$, where $X_w$ is its count.
- $\mathbb{E}$ subtracts what a **random sequence** with the same base frequencies would give.
- Dividing by $n(n-1)$, with $n$ the number of $k$-mer positions, scales the score to **0 to 1**.

## The algorithm

<div class="algo">
<p class="algo-cap"><b>Algorithm 1</b> D<sub>2</sub><sup>R</sup> of a sequence</p>
<p><b>Input:</b> sequence <i>S</i> of length <i>L</i>, word size <i>k</i></p>
<p><b>Output:</b> score <i>D</i> of <i>S</i></p>
<ol>
<li><span><i>n</i> ← <i>L</i> − <i>k</i> + 1</span><span class="cm">▷ number of <i>k</i>-mer positions</span></li>
<li><span><i>X</i> ← count of every <i>k</i>-mer of <i>S</i></span><span class="cm">▷ one pass, hash table</span></li>
<li><span><i>s</i> ← Σ<sub><i>w</i></sub> <i>X<sub>w</sub></i> (<i>X<sub>w</sub></i> − 1)</span><span class="cm">▷ matching pairs</span></li>
<li><span><i>E</i> ← expected value of <i>s</i> under the background model</span></li>
<li><span><b>return</b> <i>D</i> = (<i>s</i> − <i>E</i>) / (<i>n</i> (<i>n</i> − 1))</span></li>
</ol>
</div>

## Scanning a genome

<div class="algo">
<p class="algo-cap"><b>Algorithm 2</b> sliding-window scan</p>
<p><b>Input:</b> genome <i>G</i>, window length <i>W</i>, word size <i>k</i></p>
<p><b>Output:</b> score of every window</p>
<ol>
<li><span><i>X</i> ← count of every <i>k</i>-mer in the first window</span></li>
<li><span><i>s</i> ← Σ<sub><i>w</i></sub> <i>X<sub>w</sub></i> (<i>X<sub>w</sub></i> − 1)</span></li>
<li><span><b>for</b> each shift of the window by one base <b>do</b></span></li>
<li class="i1"><span><i>u</i> ← <i>k</i>-mer that leaves; &nbsp;<i>v</i> ← <i>k</i>-mer that enters</span></li>
<li class="i1"><span><i>s</i> ← <i>s</i> − 2 (<i>X<sub>u</sub></i> − 1); &nbsp;<i>X<sub>u</sub></i> ← <i>X<sub>u</sub></i> − 1</span><span class="cm">▷ remove <i>u</i></span></li>
<li class="i1"><span><i>s</i> ← <i>s</i> + 2 <i>X<sub>v</sub></i>; &nbsp;<i>X<sub>v</sub></i> ← <i>X<sub>v</sub></i> + 1</span><span class="cm">▷ add <i>v</i></span></li>
<li class="i1"><span>output (<i>s</i> − <i>E</i>) / (<i>n</i> (<i>n</i> − 1))</span><span class="cm">▷ <i>E</i> for this window</span></li>
<li><span><b>end for</b></span></li>
</ol>
</div>

Each shift of the window costs **constant time**, so a genome is scanned in **linear time**.

## Results

- **Simulated repeats:** AUC of about **0.99** at $k = 7$, robust for $k$ from 5 to 10.
- **Genomes:** CRISPR arrays show up as **sharp peaks**. All **three** known CRISPR regions of *A. fulgidus* are found.
- **Raw reads:** on 20 human gut metagenomes, filtering reads with D2R before the CRISPR finder **Crass** cut run time by **50 to 83 percent** and kept about **90 percent** of the CRISPRs.

## Run it

```
D2Rscanner genome.fa 1000 7
```

Three interfaces: a **Java** read filter, a **C++** genome scanner and a **Python** module.

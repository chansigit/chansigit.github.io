## The cost of releasing the brakes

- **Checkpoint blockade**, such as anti-CTLA-4, frees the immune system to attack a tumor.
- In many patients it also attacks the gut. **Colitis** is the side effect that most often forces therapy to stop.
- Nobody knew **which cells** drive it, so there was no way to prevent it without giving up the treatment.

## Tracing the damage to its source

<figure class="tall">
<img src="/assets/img/ctla4/fig2d-sources.webp" alt="Expression of Il1b, Cxcl2, Ifng and Tnf projected onto the cell map, each marking a different region." />
<figcaption>Mapping each inflammatory signal back to the cells that make it: <strong>Il1b</strong> and <strong>Cxcl2</strong> come from the invading myeloid cells, <strong>Ifng</strong> from lymphocytes, <strong>Tnf</strong> from both. ## A conversation that turns hostile

<figure class="wide">
<img src="/assets/img/ctla4/fig2g-crosstalk.webp" alt="Circular plots of significant cytokine and chemokine ligand-receptor links between clusters in the four groups; the toxic group has by far the most." />
<figcaption>Scoring every ligand–receptor pair between populations against shuffled labels: under toxicity, <strong>IL-1</strong>, <strong>TNF</strong> and <strong>CXCL9/10</strong> signaling between cell types multiplies. ## The gut spills into the blood

<figure class="wide">
<img src="/assets/img/ctla4/fig3b-serum.webp" alt="Serum concentrations of G-CSF, IFN-gamma, IL-6 and TNF-alpha across the four groups, all highest in the toxic group." />
<figcaption>Three cytokines rise in <strong>both</strong> colon and blood: <strong>IL-6</strong>, <strong>IFN-γ</strong> and <strong>G-CSF</strong> — a circulating signature of a local injury, and all three are known drivers of myeloid cell production. Zhou et al., <i>J. Exp. Med.</i> 2023 (CC BY-NC-SA 4.0), Fig. 3B.</figcaption>
</figure>

## The bone marrow joins in

<figure class="wide">
<img src="/assets/img/ctla4/fig3def-myelopoiesis.webp" alt="Neutrophil and monocyte counts in spleen, flow plots of marrow progenitors, and counts of stem and myeloid progenitor cells, all raised in the toxic group." />
<figcaption>Those cytokines push the <strong>bone marrow</strong> to make more myeloid cells: stem cells and <strong>myeloid progenitors</strong> expand, feeding neutrophils and monocytes into the circulation. The gut injury becomes a self-sustaining loop. <i>J. Exp. Med.</i> 2023 (CC BY-NC-SA 4.0), Fig. 3D–F.</figcaption>
</figure>

## Susceptibility lives in the microbiome

<figure class="tall">
<img src="/assets/img/ctla4/fig4dgh-fmt.webp" alt="Fecal transplant design, colitis scores and colonic immune cell counts: normal mice given susceptible-mouse feces develop colitis and lose tumor control." />
<figcaption>Transplanting a susceptible mouse's gut bacteria into a <strong>normal</strong> one transfers the whole phenotype: colitis, and tumors that stop responding. Susceptibility lives partly <strong>in the microbiome</strong>. <i>J. Exp. Med.</i> 2023 (CC BY-NC-SA 4.0), Fig. 4D,G,H.</figcaption>

## An ordinary gut infection is enough

<figure class="tall">
<img src="/assets/img/ctla4/fig6-infection.webp" alt="Infection experiment: timeline, weight loss, colon histology, bacterial spread to spleen and liver, and raised cytokines in infected mice on anti-CTLA-4." />
<figcaption>A normal mouse with an acute intestinal infection, treated with anti-CTLA-4, develops the <strong>same toxicity</strong>: weight loss, colon damage, bacteria escaping the gut, IL-6 up. Any disturbance of gut homeostasis opens the door. <i>J. Exp. Med.</i> 2023 (CC BY-NC-SA 4.0), Fig. 6.</figcaption>
</figure>

## Blocking IL-6 helps twice

<figure class="wide">
<img src="/assets/img/ctla4/fig5-therapy.webp" alt="Treatment timeline, tumor growth, tumor immune cells, colon histology and colitis scores: adding IL-6 blockade and antibiotics shrinks tumors and nearly removes colitis." />
<figcaption>Blocking <strong>IL-6</strong> and clearing the dysbiotic flora <strong>removed the colitis</strong> and <strong>restored tumor control</strong>. <i>J. Exp. Med.</i> 2023 (CC BY-NC-SA 4.0), Fig. 5A–E.</figcaption>
</figure>
